# Research Dim 2 — OA, Effusion, Synovitis, Baker's Cyst, Contusion vs Fracture (planes: sagittal, coronal PD FS, axial PD FS)

## 1. Knee osteoarthritis on MRI (medial, lateral, patellofemoral)

### Minimum positive findings (MOST MRI-based OA definition)
- **"MOST simple" definition**: cartilage damage WORMS ≥2 (at least a partial-thickness focal cartilage defect) PLUS a definite osteophyte (score ≥2). (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10922537/)
- Delphi panel (more specific): (1) definite osteophyte (grade ≥2) AND full-thickness cartilage loss (WORMS 2.5/5/6) tibiofemoral; or (2) either definite osteophyte OR full-thickness loss PLUS two of: subchondral BML or cyst, meniscal subluxation/maceration/degenerative tear, partial-thickness cartilage loss, bone attrition; (3) patellofemoral: definite osteophyte PLUS partial (grade 2–4) or full-thickness (grade 5–6) cartilage loss.
- "Definite cartilage damage" = WORMS ≥2 (at least partial-thickness focal defect) or extending to bone (2.5/5/6). (MOST definition paper: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10361157/)
- Per-feature thresholds: cartilage — modified Outerbridge grade ≥2 = focal partial-thickness defect (fissuring/ulceration <50% depth = grade 2; ≥50% depth without bone exposure = grade 3; full-thickness loss with bone exposure = grade 4). Osteophyte — definite marginal or central (WORMS ≥2; equivocal = WORMS 1 = negative); size grading: grade 1 minimal <3 mm, grade 2 moderate 3–5 mm, grade 3 severe >5 mm (Zhang et al., https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6235223/). Subchondral change (supportive, not required): BMLs (ill-defined high signal on PD/T2 FS), subchondral cysts, sclerosis, attrition; MOAKS BML size by % subregion volume (1 <33%, 2 33–66%, 3 >66%).
- **Competition host criteria (discussion/733343): OA = ≥1 cm of high-grade (>50% thickness) cartilage loss per compartment; borderline findings graded negative.**

### Scoring systems
- WORMS: cartilage 0–6 per subregion (0 normal; 1 = intrachondral signal change only, normal thickness; 2 = focal partial-thickness defect <1 cm … 6 = diffuse ≥75% full-thickness loss); osteophytes 0–7 (1 = equivocal); BMLs/cysts/attrition 0–3; effusion-synovitis 0–3.
- MOAKS: BML by subregion volume thresholds 33%/66%; osteophytes 0–3 (small/medium/large, scored by protuberance) at 12 locations; Hoffa-synovitis 0–3; effusion-synovitis 0–3. BMLs on T2/intermediate/PD FS or STIR all three planes; osteophytes best on non-FS short-TE (T1 > PD) or GRE-type. (Hunter et al. 2011, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4058435/)
- KL correlation: KL ≥2 (definite osteophyte) = conventional structural OA threshold; KL 1 = negative. MRI detects osteophytes more often than radiographs. KL 2 WITH joint-space narrowing has far more cartilage loss (44% WORMS ≥4) than KL 2 without (4%) — JSN tracks cartilage loss; osteophyte alone often doesn't. (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3653624/)

### Best planes per surface
- Coronal (PD FS): medial/lateral tibiofemoral weight-bearing cartilage (femoral condyles + tibial plateaus), marginal osteophytes of condyles/plateaus, tibial spines, notch osteophytes, meniscal extrusion.
- Axial (PD FS): patellofemoral — patellar facets, trochlear groove, patellar and PF margin osteophytes; suprapatellar pouch (effusion grading). Osteophyte mapping: axial = patellar margins, PF facets, condyle/notch; coronal = condyles, plateaus, tibial spine; sagittal = patellar poles, anterior femur, posterior condyles/plateaus. (OAI osteophyte study, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9718261/)
- Sagittal: trochlea/anterior femur, patellar poles, posterior femoral condyles and posterior tibial plateau osteophytes, central weight-bearing cartilage. PD is most accurate sequence for chondral lesions.
- Caveat: osteophytes are less conspicuous on FS sequences (marrow fat suppressed) — appear as cortical/marginal spurs; coronal PD FS = workhorse for tibiofemoral margins, axial for PF margins.

### Borderline findings that stay NEGATIVE
- WORMS cartilage grade 1 (signal change only, normal thickness) — below ≥2 threshold.
- WORMS osteophyte grade 1 ("equivocal"); KL-1-equivalent.
- Tiny marginal osteophytes <3 mm in isolation (esp. patellar poles/tibial spines, older or athletic knees) — osteophytes common in asymptomatic people.
- Small physiologic joint fluid; Hoffa fat pad signal alone (sensitive but non-specific).

## 2. Joint effusion
- ACLOAS quantitative scheme: max AP diameter of suprapatellar recess on midline sagittal FS: grade 0 <2 mm; 1 = ≥2 to <5 mm; 2 = ≥5 to <10 mm; 3 = ≥10 mm. (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7677197/)
- MOAKS ordinal on axial FS: 0 physiologic; 1 small (fluid continuously into retropatellar space); 2 medium (slight convexity of suprapatellar bursa); 3 large (capsular distension). (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6310094/)
- Ultrasound cutoff for pathologic effusion: suprapatellar pouch depth ≥4 mm (EULAR context).
- Best plane/sequence: axial PD FS through suprapatellar pouch (grading); sagittal FS for AP-diameter measurement. Fluid uniformly very high signal on PD FS/T2 FS; MRI most accurate modality; suprapatellar pouch most common site (~51%).
- Actionable: effusion positive when suprapatellar AP ≥2 mm on sagittal or fluid continuously fills retropatellar space on axial PD FS. Competition host criteria: effusion = moderate or large (i.e., MOAKS 2–3 / ≥5 mm).
- Note: on sagittal, the suprapatellar pouch extends ABOVE the patella — a tight 130 mm crop centered on the joint line can clip the superior pouch and underestimate effusion.

## 3. Synovitis vs effusion on NON-CONTRAST MRI
- Central limitation: on non-contrast MRI, synovial thickening and uncomplicated effusion CANNOT be reliably separated — both hyperintense on PD FS/T2 FS. Hence the OA literature's composite term "effusion-synovitis." (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6504589/)
- Contrast-enhanced MRI = reference standard (OMERACT: above-normal post-Gd enhancement thicker than normal synovium; inflamed synovium enhances, static effusion does not).
- Reliability: Hoffa fat pad signal change on non-contrast MRI = sensitive (86–97%) but very poorly specific (10–38%) vs CE-MRI (Roemer et al.); Loeuille et al.: only CE-MRI correlated with microscopically proven synovitis — NO correlation for non-contrast MRI. PD-FS vs CE-T1-FS only fair-to-moderate agreement for PF synovitis.
- Surrogates on non-contrast: Hoffa-synovitis (MOAKS 0–3; size ≤33% / 34–66% / ≥66% of fat pad; signal vs cartilage/fluid); frond-like/villous, nodular or irregular INTERMEDIATE-signal (lower than fluid, ~cartilage signal) thickening lining the joint; mass-like suprapatellar pouch wall thickening. Synovial thickness scoring reliability much worse on non-contrast than CE.
- Emerging non-contrast techniques (not in dataset): quantitative DESS with fluid nulling, double inversion recovery (overestimates synovial volume ~17.5%), FLAIR-FS.
- Can both be independently present? YES — synovial hypertrophy and effusion frequently coexist; separable on CE-MRI, merged into one bright/heterogeneous complex on non-contrast. Large effusion with smooth thin walls → uncomplicated fluid; effusion + irregular thickened walls/fronds → coexisting synovitis.
- Mimics: synovial plica (normal thin <3 mm low-signal band on axial PD FS in medial gutter; pathologic ≥3 mm, intermediate/high signal, contacting MFC, may have adjacent cartilage wear; confirm on two planes); PVNS/TGCT (nodular synovial proliferation LOW-to-intermediate on both T1 and T2, GRE/T2* blooming — nearly pathognomonic; may erode bone both sides); lipoma arborescens (frond-like villous mass following fat signal, suppresses on FS, chronic effusion); post-surgical synovial change (irregular intermediate-signal thickening ± susceptibility artifact; indistinguishable from recurrent synovitis without contrast/history); intra-articular bodies, rice bodies, synovial chondromatosis.

## 4. Baker's (popliteal) cyst
- Location: fluid distension of gastrocnemius–semimembranosus bursa, posteromedial popliteal fossa, via valvular slit between medial head of gastrocnemius and semimembranosus tendon. Small fluid here is physiologic and not pathologic alone.
- FOV/crop: cyst neck/origin sits immediately posteromedial to medial femoral condyle at joint line — well inside standard knee FOV. ACR caps knee FOV at 16 cm; typical protocols 140–160 mm FOV, 3–4 mm slices. → posteromedial bursa region essentially always within even a tight central crop of axial/coronal FOV. Caveat (inference): only the tail of a very large/ruptured cyst dissecting distally into calf may run off the inferior end of the axial stack; the diagnostic neck remains in-plane. On sagittal series the cyst lies at the posterior FOV edge — a 130 mm crop could clip it there.
- Best plane/sequence: axial PD FS / T2 FS — diagnostic neck between semimembranosus tendon and medial gastrocnemius head ("speech-bubble"/"talk-bubble" configuration) — required for definitive diagnosis vs other popliteal fossa collections. Sagittal FS: craniocaudal extent and rupture (fluid tracking along calf fascial planes). Cyst content: uniformly high T2/PD FS, low T1; may contain debris/septations/loose bodies. Rupture: distal fluid + surrounding edema — clinical DVT mimic.
- Mimics (cyst/cyst-like review: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3675245/): popliteal artery aneurysm (variable signal by flow, laminated thrombus, rim calcification, continuity with artery, flow artifact — critical don't-miss mimic); popliteal vein varices; ganglion cysts (no semimembranosus–gastrocnemius neck; e.g., proximal tibiofibular joint, parameniscal); hematoma (age-dependent signal, may be T1-bright, no neck); abscess (surrounding inflammatory change); popliteal lymph nodes (fatty hilum); other bursae (pes anserine, semimembranosus-TCL — different locations).
- Competition host criteria: Baker's = moderate or large.

## 5. Bone contusion vs occult fracture
Terminology: bone contusion = marrow edema-like signal WITHOUT contour deformity or discrete fracture line; subchondral fracture = line not violating articular surface; osteochondral fracture = contour deformity or line disrupting cartilage + subchondral plate. "Edema-like" = ill-defined high signal on fluid-sensitive sequences that does NOT completely replace marrow fat on T1. (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7571512/)

| Feature | Bone contusion (bone bruise) | Occult/trabecular fracture |
|---|---|---|
| T1 non-FS | Geographic, non-linear, ill-defined low/intermediate signal; fat not fully replaced (Mink & Deutsch) | Linear or curvilinear low-signal line (band of low signal on both T1 and T2), ± speckled low signal |
| PD/T2 FS, STIR | Ill-defined high signal, fading margins | High-signal edema surrounding a line (low or high signal); line parallel to subchondral plate in SIF |
| Cortex/contour | Intact cortex, no depression | Cortical breach, step-off, or subchondral impaction/depression |

Sources: bone bruise review https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4340462/; AJR bone marrow https://www.ajronline.org/doi/10.2214/AJR.10.4961; Jarraya occult fracture review https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3613077/
- Classic ACL-injury contusion pattern: lateral femoral condyle sulcus + posterolateral tibial plateau — contusion, not fracture, unless line/depression present. Patellar dislocation pattern: medial patellar facet + lateral femoral condyle.
- Vellet classification of occult post-traumatic lesions: reticular (serpiginous, distant from plate — least severe), geographic I/II (confluent, adjacent to plate), linear subcortical (~2 mm wide), plus stress and osteochondral types.
- **T1 non-FS is the key fracture-line sequence**: "T1W images should be obtained to detect fracture lines"; PD less reliable for the line than T1. (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7571514/)
- RSNA-relevant caveat: with only PD FS planes (no T1 guaranteed), you lose the most specific fracture-line sequence — fracture may be INFERRED only from a low-signal line within PD-FS edema visible in two planes, cortical step-off, or subchondral impaction; otherwise label contusion/edema.
- GRE/T2*/SWI: susceptibility-sensitive; amplify trabecular discontinuity/hemorrhage; useful adjunct for occult lines and PVNS; not standard in dataset.
- Subchondral insufficiency fracture (SIFK, formerly SONK): subchondral T2-hypointense line (thin, often discontinuous, parallel to and just below the plate) + extensive BME-like signal, typically weight-bearing MFC in older adults; frequently with medial meniscus posterior root tears. Differentiate from osteonecrosis (curvilinear serpiginous boundary engaging the plate, double-line sign). Subchondral low-signal area >4 mm thick or >14 mm long = poor prognosis. (Ochi 2022 https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9068663/; Malghem 2023 https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10545656/)
- Stress/fatigue fractures: linear low-T1 line perpendicular to trabeculae/cortex + periosteal edema (grading: periosteal edema → marrow edema on T2 → edema on T1/PD → discrete line).
- Confirmation practice: fracture line should be visible on ≥2 contiguous slices (3–4 mm) AND confirmed in a second orthogonal plane before calling fracture rather than contusion; single-slice single-plane line = likely volume averaging/artifact. Contusions show NO line in ANY plane. Cortical breaches best confirmed on plane perpendicular to cortex (coronal for tibial plateau rims; sagittal for anterior/posterior plateau; axial for patella).
- Chronicity: contusion signal appears ~30 h post-injury; early literature suggested 8–12 weeks resolution but prospective data: median healing 42 weeks (Boks AJR 2007, 157 bruises); reticular fastest; prolonged by OA and age; some persist up to 2 years; 80% shrink >50% by 12–14 weeks. Healing/subacute: decreasing edema; low-signal sclerosis/callus line (can make fracture line MORE visible subacutely); persistent subchondral low signal + collapse → progression to SIF-ON rather than healing contusion. Stress-reaction edema usually resolves within ~6 months.

## Quick implementation table
| Finding | Positive call | Stay negative | Best plane (this dataset) |
|---|---|---|---|
| OA | Cartilage defect ≥ partial thickness (WORMS ≥2) or definite osteophyte (≥2–3 mm spur), ideally both; supporting BML/cyst/attrition. Competition: ≥1 cm of >50% cartilage loss per compartment | WORMS 1 signal-only change; equivocal/<3 mm osteophyte | Coronal PD FS (TF), axial PD FS (PF), sagittal (trochlea/posterior margins) |
| Effusion | Suprapatellar AP ≥2 mm sagittal, or retropatellar fluid on axial; grade 1 = 2–5, 2 = 5–10, 3 = ≥10 mm. Competition: moderate/large | Trace physiologic fluid | Axial PD FS (grading), sagittal (measurement) |
| Synovitis (non-contrast) | Frond-like/nodular intermediate-signal synovial thickening; Hoffa hyperintensity — label cautiously, specificity 10–38% | Uniform fluid signal alone = effusion only; "effusion-synovitis" when inseparable | Axial + sagittal PD FS |
| Baker cyst | Posteromedial fluid with neck between semimembranosus & medial gastrocnemius on axial; competition: moderate/large | Small physiologic bursal fluid; vessels/aneurysm (flow/continuity), ganglion (no neck) | Axial PD FS (neck), sagittal (extent); origin within 140–160 mm FOV |
| Fracture vs contusion | Low-signal line (esp. T1; on PD FS if visible), cortical breach, subchondral impaction — confirm ≥2 slices, 2 planes | Geographic non-linear edema without line/deformity = contusion | All three PD FS planes; absence of T1 non-FS limits specificity |
