# RSNA Knee MRI Abnormality Detection: Per-Finding Diagnostic Guide and Anatomical Routing Table

*Technical reference for the Kaggle RSNA Knee Abnormality Detection competition — MRI planes, sequences, anatomical targets, crop and slice rules, and label criteria for all 12 findings.*

---

# Section 1: Competition Context

The RSNA Knee Abnormality Detection challenge is **live**: launched July 30, 2026, entry/merger deadline October 15, 2026, final submission October 22, 2026, winners announced at RSNA 2026 in November. This is a code competition (no internet, ≤9 h runtime) scored by **macro-averaged ROC AUC across 12 binary targets**; external data and pretrained models are allowed (competition overview).

**Dataset.** A brand-new RSNA AI Challenge collection — explicitly **not MRNet-derived** — of >5,000 knee MRI exams from 16–19 sites across five continents, paired with original radiology reports in ~9–12 languages. Intensities, orientations, and resolutions vary across series and studies; slices per series typically 20–45 (median 30), with a long tail. DICOMs use mixed transfer syntaxes and an 86-tag allowlist. **Critical label facts: only 58 of 4,407 train studies carry per-condition labels**; the other 4,349 have report text only, so the core challenge is mining labels from multilingual free text. The ~1,300 test studies have **no reports at all**. Series metadata gives only plane (Sagittal/Coronal/Axial) plus Fluid_Sensitive and Fat_Suppression flags — **no named sequences** (no "sagittal T1" or "coronal PD FS"). Community EDA counts ~9,864 sagittal / 8,609 coronal / 5,898 axial series (official data page; dim03).

**Official 12-label taxonomy** (matches this document's finding list exactly): ACL, MCL, Medial Meniscus, Lateral Meniscus, Medial OA, Lateral OA, PF OA, Effusion, Synovitis, Baker's, Contusion, Fracture. No umbrella "abnormal" label; no PCL/LCL targets.

**Host-posted annotation criteria (discussion/733343)**, double-read by MSK radiologists with third-reader adjudication:
- ACL = high-grade partial (>50% fibers) or full tear
- MCL = high-grade acute tear
- Meniscus = surface-touching signal on ≥2 images OR morphologic deformity
- OA = ≥1 cm of high-grade (>50% thickness) cartilage loss per compartment
- Effusion and Baker's cyst = moderate or large
- **Borderline findings are graded negative** — bias your decision thresholds toward specificity.

**Community practices (early-competition, NOT validated winning solutions — none exist yet).** Physical-scale central crops of 130–160 mm resized to a fixed pixel grid (e.g., 130 mm → 336 px ≈ 0.387 mm/px), because naive resizing of larger FOVs can erase 1–3 mm pathology; the forum warns to **verify the crop actually fits the array or the operation silently becomes a no-op** (discussion 734105; LB 0.768–0.894 baselines). Slice sorting must use **ImagePositionPatient · row/column vectors from ImageOrientationPatient, never filename**. No Laterality column exists — derive laterality from image-center geometry (flip coronal/axial horizontally, reverse sagittal stack order); this matters because 5 of 12 targets are medial/lateral pairs. Common architectures: per-plane encoders with cross-plane fusion into 12 sigmoid heads; DINOv2 small/base is the dominant backbone; MRNet is raised as external data. A metadata-only probe reaches ~0.65 macro AUC random-fold but 0.598 scanner-grouped — no metadata shortcut.

**Transferable evidence — MRNet / Bien et al. 2018 (PLoS Medicine).** 1,370 Stanford exams; protocol coronal T1, coronal T2 FS, sagittal PD, sagittal T2 FS, axial PD FS; the model used one series per plane (sagittal T2, coronal T1, axial PD). Slices per series 17–61, mean 31.5 ± 8.0. Per-slice AlexNet → max-pool over slices → logistic-regression fusion. Plane-importance findings: **axial PD most beneficial for abnormality and meniscal tear; coronal T1 for ACL tear** (AUCs 0.937 abnormal / 0.965 ACL / 0.847 meniscal). Takeaway: no single plane dominates; fuse planes, and expect ~20–45 usable slices per series.

# Section 2: Ten Highest-Priority Questions

## 1. MCL — Is coronal fat-suppressed imaging essential, and does a central crop risk cutting the ligament?

**Yes, coronal FS PD/T2 is effectively essential, and yes, a 130 mm central crop can clip the distal ligament.**

- Coronal FS PD/T2 shows the MCL along its long axis — periligamentous edema, superficial/deep layer involvement, discontinuity — and is the highest-value sequence; sagittal is poor for a structure this thin in-plane. Axial FS PD/T2 is complementary for injury level and the posteromedial corner. If fat suppression fails, grade I and small deep-layer lesions lose conspicuity (PMC3548666).
- Anatomy vs crop: the superficial MCL originates ~31.1 ± 4.6 mm above the femoral joint line and inserts ~62.4 ± 5.5 mm below the tibial joint line (centroid range ~54.7–71.5 mm; mean length ~100.7 mm; Liu et al.). A 130 mm crop centered on the joint line reaches only ~65 mm each way — it barely covers the tibial insertion centroid and can clip the distal attachment footprint, periligamentous edema, and larger or off-center knees. An in-plane 130 mm crop can also trim the subcutaneous medial soft tissues where the MCL lies.
- **ML rule: preserve the full medial femoral epicondyle, the medial joint line, and ≥75 mm below the medial tibial plateau; verify the crop mask against DICOM PixelSpacing rather than assuming a central 130 mm crop is safe.** The MCL target is a high-grade acute tear (grade II–III); grade I sprains stay negative (host criteria, discussion/733343).

## 2. Synovitis — Can it be reliably distinguished from uncomplicated effusion on non-contrast MRI?

**No — not reliably. Treat Synovitis as a low-confidence, high-uncertainty label.**

- On non-contrast MRI, synovial thickening and simple effusion are both bright on PD FS/T2 FS; the OA literature's composite term "effusion-synovitis" exists precisely because they merge (PMC6504589). Contrast-enhanced MRI is the reference standard: inflamed synovium enhances, static fluid does not.
- Quantitative evidence: Hoffa fat-pad signal change on non-contrast MRI is sensitive (86–97%) but **very poorly specific (10–38%)** against CE-MRI (Roemer et al.); Loeuille et al. found only CE-MRI correlated with microscopically proven synovitis — no correlation for non-contrast MRI.
- Practical heuristic when no contrast exists: frond-like, villous, nodular, or irregular **intermediate** signal (lower than fluid, ~cartilage signal) lining the joint, or mass-like suprapatellar wall thickening, suggests synovitis; smooth thin-walled uniform fluid does not. Do not over-trigger: the host counts synovitis as its own target, so calibrate with the 58 labeled studies and expect noisy positives.

## 3. Lateral meniscus — Which sagittal/coronal regions identify body, horn, and root tears, and how many contiguous abnormal slices are expected?

**Map regions to planes, and require surface-touching signal on ≥2 images (or definite morphology) — matching the host rule.**

- Region-by-plane mapping: **body** — peripheral sagittal slices (continuous "bow-tie," ~2 contiguous bow-ties at 4–5 mm thickness) and mid-coronal (triangular body, truncation, extrusion); **anterior horn** — central-anterior sagittal, anterior coronal, axial; **posterior horn** — central-posterior sagittal, posterior coronal, axial; **roots** — posterior/central coronal (truncation sign), central sagittal (ghost meniscus), thin axial (radial cleft). Axial is best for mapping radial, root, and parrot-beak tears (dim01).
- **Two-slice-touch rule (De Smet & Tuite 2006):** surface-contacting signal in the same region on two images (classically two consecutive sagittal slices; one sagittal + one coronal acceptable) gives ~90% PPV versus ~53% on a single sagittal image. The host codifies this: signal touching a surface on ≥2 images OR morphologic deformity.
- Exceptions: root tears may be conspicuous on only 1–2 key slices per plane — morphology (ghost, truncation, radial cleft, extrusion; lateral extrusion threshold ~1 mm) overrides slice count. Lateral-specific mimics: popliteus tendon, meniscofemoral ligaments (Humphrey/Wrisberg), transverse intermeniscal ligament, magic angle, discoid meniscus (≥3 consecutive 5 mm bow-ties; diffuse signal alone ≠ tear).

## 4. Lateral OA — Which cartilage surfaces and osteophytes define a positive case, and which planes show them best?

**Positive = ≥1 cm of >50%-thickness cartilage loss in the lateral tibiofemoral compartment (host rule); coronal PD FS is the workhorse plane; borderline findings stay negative.**

- Surfaces: lateral femoral condyle and lateral tibial plateau weight-bearing cartilage; marginal osteophytes of the lateral condyle/plateau and tibial spines. Coronal PD FS covers the tibiofemoral weight-bearing cartilage and marginal osteophytes; sagittal adds posterior condyle/plateau margins and central weight-bearing cartilage; axial is for patellofemoral disease, not this target (dim02; OAI osteophyte study).
- Thresholding: the host criterion (≥1 cm of high-grade >50% cartilage loss per compartment) is stricter than the MOST MRI definition (focal partial-thickness cartilage defect WORMS ≥2 plus a definite osteophyte). Under the host rule, a definite osteophyte (WORMS ≥2, ~≥3 mm spur) supports but does not alone satisfy positivity. Note osteophytes are **less conspicuous on FS sequences** (marrow fat suppressed) — read them as cortical/marginal spurs.
- Stay negative: WORMS cartilage grade 1 (signal-only, normal thickness), equivocal osteophytes (WORMS 1), isolated <3 mm marginal spurs, small physiologic fluid. Joint-space narrowing tracks cartilage loss (KL 2 with JSN: 44% WORMS ≥4 cartilage vs 4% without) — use it as a supporting cue, not a primary one.

## 5. Baker's cyst — Does the entire posteromedial region need to remain in the crop?

**The diagnostic neck always lies within a 140–160 mm FOV; only the tails of large/ruptured cysts risk clipping — mainly at the sagittal posterior edge or axial inferior end.**

- The cyst's neck sits between the semimembranosus tendon and medial gastrocnemius head, immediately posteromedial to the medial femoral condyle at the joint line — inside even a tight central crop of axial/coronal FOV (ACR caps knee FOV at 16 cm; typical 140–160 mm).
- Risk zones: on **sagittal** series the cyst sits near the posterior FOV edge, so a 130 mm crop can clip it; the tail of a large or ruptured cyst dissecting into the calf can run off the inferior end of the axial stack. The diagnostic neck remains in-plane regardless.
- **ML rule:** diagnose on axial PD FS via the "speech-bubble" neck configuration (required to exclude mimics — especially popliteal artery aneurysm, the critical don't-miss mimic); use sagittal for craniocaudal extent and rupture. The host counts only moderate-or-large cysts, so small physiologic bursal fluid stays negative — the cases that matter are exactly the ones large enough to be visible even if the tail is clipped.

## 6. ACL — Is continuity across several adjacent sagittal slices necessary, rather than one strongly abnormal slice?

**No formal two-slice rule exists. One unequivocal discontinuity or nonvisualization can be positive; signal abnormality on a single slice is never enough.**

- The normal ACL spans only ~2–3 contiguous sagittal (or coronal) slices at 3–4 mm thickness, so "several abnormal slices" is not a requirement (Ng et al.).
- Positive-level evidence: fiber discontinuity or fluid-filled gap, diffuse enlargement with cloud-like FS hyperintensity plus lost morphology, wavy/abnormal contour, abnormal slope vs Blumensaat's line, nonvisualization/empty notch, or avulsion. Complete discontinuity confirmed in **at least two image planes** is the strongest criterion (Dove et al.).
- Single-slice **signal** alone is insufficient — partial-volume averaging at the femoral attachment, normal distal striation, mucoid degeneration ("celery-stalk," preserved fibers, no discontinuity), and short-TE effects all mimic it. When sagittal is equivocal, confirm on coronal/axial or add morphology plus secondary signs (anterior tibial translation ~≥5 mm, pivot-shift contusion pattern, deep lateral femoral notch — supportive only, never stand-alone).
- Host rule: positive = high-grade partial (>50% fibers) or full tear; bundle-level partial grading is unreliable on routine MRI, so low-grade partials and equivocal cases go negative.

## 7. Contusion vs fracture — Which sequences and signs separate marrow edema from an occult fracture?

**The T1 non-FS linear low-signal line is the key discriminator — but it may not exist in this dataset; fall back to cortical breach, subchondral impaction, or a line visible in ≥2 slices and 2 planes; geographic non-linear edema is contusion.**

- T1 non-FS is the key fracture-line sequence ("T1W images should be obtained to detect fracture lines"; PD is less reliable for the line). Contusion: geographic, non-linear, ill-defined low/intermediate T1 signal that does not fully replace marrow fat (Mink & Deutsch). Fracture: linear/curvilinear low-signal line on both T1 and T2, ± cortical breach, step-off, or subchondral impaction/depression.
- Dataset caveat: only plane + fluid-sensitivity/fat-suppression flags are provided and T1 is not guaranteed; with PD FS planes only, infer fracture from a low-signal line within PD-FS edema confirmed in **≥2 contiguous slices AND a second orthogonal plane**, cortical step-off, or subchondral impaction — otherwise label Contusion (dim02). A single-slice, single-plane line is likely volume averaging or artifact.
- Edema-pattern priors: the classic ACL-injury pattern (lateral femoral condyle sulcus + posterolateral tibial plateau) is contusion unless a line or depression exists. Watch for subchondral insufficiency fracture (SIFK): thin subchondral T2-hypointense line parallel to the plate + extensive edema, typically weight-bearing MFC in older adults, often with medial posterior root tears — differentiate from osteonecrosis (serpiginous curvilinear boundary engaging the plate, double-line sign). Subchondral low-signal >4 mm thick or >14 mm long carries poor prognosis (Ochi 2022; Malghem 2023).

## 8. Effusion vs synovitis — Can both be independently present, and what evidence differentiates them?

**Yes, they frequently coexist — and on non-contrast MRI they merge into one bright complex; morphology of the fluid's walls is the only practical differentiator.**

- Separable on CE-MRI (enhancing synovium vs non-enhancing fluid); merged on the non-contrast PD FS/T2 FS available here — hence "effusion-synovitis."
- Uncomplicated effusion: large fluid volume with **smooth, thin, uniform walls**. Coexisting synovitis: **irregular, frond-like, or nodular intermediate-signal** (fluid-intermediate, ~cartilage signal) thickening of the recess walls, or Hoffa fat-pad hyperintensity (sensitive 86–97%, specific only 10–38%).
- Effusion grading for the host's moderate-or-large threshold: sagittal suprapatellar AP diameter ≥5 mm (ACLOAS grade 2–3: 5–10 mm / ≥10 mm) or MOAKS axial grade 2–3 (suprapatellar bursa convexity → capsular distension). Crop note: the suprapatellar pouch extends above the patella — a tight 130 mm joint-line-centered crop on sagittal can clip the pouch and underestimate effusion.
- Mimics to exclude before calling synovitis: plica (thin <3 mm medial-gutter band; pathologic ≥3 mm contacting MFC), PVNS/TGCT (low-to-intermediate on T1 and T2, GRE blooming), lipoma arborescens (fat-signal fronds that suppress on FS), post-surgical synovial change.

## 9. Meniscal degeneration vs tear — Should intrameniscal signal that does not reach an articular surface count as negative?

**Yes — unequivocally negative. Grade 1/2 intrasubstance signal is degeneration, not tear, and this matches the host rule exactly.**

- Grade 1 = globular intrameniscal signal, no surface contact; grade 2 = linear intrameniscal signal, no definite surface contact; grade 3 = signal reaches a surface. Grades 1–2 represent intrasubstance/myxoid degeneration (and normal vascularity in children) and are negative for "tear" (PMC7878874).
- Host criteria: positive requires signal **touching a surface on ≥2 images OR morphologic deformity**; borderline findings are graded negative. So if you cannot tell whether signal reaches a surface, the high-specificity call — and the host-consistent call — is **no definite tear**.
- Practical: morphology (truncation, surface defect, displaced fragment, root ghost/truncation) can override the signal rule; in a clearly postoperative meniscus, surface-contacting intermediate signal loses specificity — use fluid-equivalent T2 clefts or displaced fragments instead.

## 10. OA grading — What minimum cartilage loss, JSN, osteophyte, or subchondral change constitutes positive OA?

**Competition rule: ≥1 cm of >50%-thickness cartilage loss per compartment. A definite osteophyte supports; equivocal and grade-1 findings stay negative.**

- Reference frame (MOST MRI definition): definite cartilage damage = WORMS ≥2 (at least a partial-thickness focal defect), definite osteophyte = WORMS ≥2 (~≥3 mm spur); "MOST simple" OA = both. The host's ≥1 cm of >50% loss is a higher bar — focal grade-2 fissuring alone stays negative (host criteria, discussion/733343).
- **Osteophytes:** definite marginal or central osteophyte counts as supporting evidence; WORMS 1 "equivocal" spurs and isolated <3 mm spurs (common at patellar poles/tibial spines, in older or athletic knees) are negative. Osteophytes are less conspicuous on FS sequences — read them on the least-suppressed series available.
- **Joint-space narrowing:** not independently scored on MRI; it tracks cartilage loss (KL 2 with JSN → 44% WORMS ≥4 cartilage vs 4% without JSN), so use it as a corroborating cue.
- **Subchondral change** (BMLs, cysts, sclerosis, attrition): supportive, never required and never sufficient alone.
- Compartment routing: medial/lateral OA on coronal PD FS; PF OA on axial PD FS (patellar facets, trochlea); sagittal for trochlea, patellar poles, posterior margins. **Stay negative:** WORMS cartilage grade 1 signal-only change, equivocal osteophytes, small physiologic fluid, isolated Hoffa signal.

# Part II — The 12 Findings: Ten Questions Each

# Section 02 — Plane & Sequence Routing: Ligaments, Menisci, Tibiofemoral OA

Dataset context for all six findings: series are labeled only by `Anatomical_Plane`, `Fluid_Sensitive`, and `Fat_Suppression` — there are no named sequences (no "sagittal T1" or "coronal PD FS"). `Fluid_Sensitive=1` implies a T2/PD/STIR-like series; `Fat_Suppression=1` implies FS PD/T2-like weighting. A non-fluid-sensitive sagittal or coronal series is your T1/PD morphology series. Typical FOV is 140–160 mm, so a 130 mm central crop trims ~5–15 mm of periphery per side — this matters differently for each finding below (host criteria, discussion/733343; discussion/734105).

### ACL tear
1. **Most diagnostic plane:** **Sagittal** (or sagittal-oblique parallel to the ligament); the normal ACL is visible on ~2–3 contiguous 3–4 mm sagittal slices (Ng et al. 2011). Sagittal shows fiber course, slope, discontinuity, and anterior tibial translation in one view.
2. **Preferred sequence:** **Fluid-sensitive FS PD/T2** (`Fluid_Sensitive=1 & Fat_Suppression=1`) — acute edema, hemorrhage, fluid-filled fiber gaps, and marrow contusions are most conspicuous there. A non-FS sagittal (T1/PD-like) is valuable for fiber morphology and chronic scarring.
3. **Confirmatory plane or sequence:** **Coronal and axial FS PD/T2** — coronal for the empty intercondylar notch/empty lateral wall and femoral avulsion; axial for the femoral origin and bundle-level partial tears. Complete discontinuity confirmed in ≥2 orthogonal planes is the robust criterion (Dove et al. 2024).
4. **Where anatomically to look:** The intercondylar notch on central sagittal slices — fibers from the posteromedial lateral femoral condyle to the anteromedial tibial spine. Compare fiber slope to **Blumensaat's line** (intercondylar roof): a normal ACL is parallel to or steeper than it; a torn distal stump lies too horizontal, a proximal stump too vertical.
5. **Crop: is a central 130 mm crop sufficient?** **Yes, conditionally.** The notch is geometrically central, so the ACL itself survives a 130 mm crop in virtually all 140–160 mm FOV acquisitions. The risk is at the edges: pivot-shift contusions (mid-lateral femoral condyle, posterolateral tibial plateau) and Segond fragments sit peripherally and can be clipped in large knees or off-center scans. Keep the full lateral femoral condyle and posterior tibia in-plane if secondary signs feed the model.
6. **Adjacent slices normally needed to confirm:** **No formal two-slice rule exists.** A single slice showing unequivocal complete discontinuity or nonvisualization suffices; signal abnormality on one slice alone does not (partial volume, normal striation, mucoid change). Confirm in a second plane or add morphology plus secondary signs (Ng et al. 2011).
7. **What constitutes a positive case:** **High-grade partial tear (>50% of fibers) or full tear** (host criteria, discussion/733343): fiber discontinuity/fluid-filled gap, diffuse cloud-like FS hyperintensity with lost morphology, wavy/lax contour, abnormal slope, or empty notch. Supportive secondary signs — anterior tibial translation ≥5 mm (≈86% sensitivity/99% specificity; >7 mm near-diagnostic), pivot-shift contusion pattern, deep lateral femoral notch ≥1.5–2 mm, uncovered posterior horn of the lateral meniscus, Segond fracture — raise confidence but never stand alone.
8. **Borderline findings that stay negative or uncertain:** Single-slice signal change; increased signal with some continuous fibers and no laxity (low-grade partial); mucoid degeneration with intact fibers; possible partial-volume effects. Host rule: borderline = negative (discussion/733343).
9. **Common mimics / false positives:** **Mucoid degeneration** ("celery-stalk": fusiform thickening, diffuse high signal, preserved parallel fibers, no discontinuity — discontinuity seen in 97% of complete tears vs 0% of mucoid cases; Celikyay et al.), **ACL ganglion** (fluid-equivalent lobulated lesion splaying but not disrupting fibers), partial-volume averaging at the femoral attachment, and normal short-TE striation.
10. **Does chronicity, degeneration, previous surgery, or image quality change interpretation?** **Yes.** Chronic tears show nonvisualization, an empty notch, or a distal stump scarred onto the PCL that falsely appears continuous; acute edema may be absent, so weight morphology over signal. Post-reconstruction grafts normally show intermediate signal during revascularization at ~4–8 months and ligamentize to low signal by ~12 (up to 24) months — do not call homogeneous early graft hyperintensity a retear; require discontinuity, abnormal orientation, or recurrent translation. Thick slices (>4 mm) inflate partial-volume false positives.

### MCL tear
1. **Most diagnostic plane:** **Coronal** — it displays the ligament along its long axis; sagittal is poor because the MCL is thin in the through-plane direction.
2. **Preferred sequence:** **Coronal FS PD/T2 is effectively essential** (`Fluid_Sensitive=1 & Fat_Suppression=1`): periligamentous edema, superficial/deep layer involvement, and discontinuity all depend on fluid contrast. If no FS coronal exists, non-FS PD/T2 shows thickening and gross discontinuity but misses grade I and small deep-layer lesions.
3. **Confirmatory plane or sequence:** **Axial FS PD/T2** — cross-sectional fiber continuity, injury level (femoral vs mid-substance vs tibial), deep MCL, posterior oblique ligament, posteromedial corner.
4. **Where anatomically to look:** Superficial MCL from the medial femoral epicondyle (centroid ~31 mm above the femoral joint line) to the medial tibia (centroid ~62 mm below the tibial joint line; range ~55–72 mm). Total centroid-to-centroid length ~100 mm; AP width ~11–18 mm (Liu et al.).
5. **Crop: is a central 130 mm crop sufficient?** **No — high risk.** A 130 mm crop centered on the joint line extends only ~65 mm each way: it barely reaches the tibial insertion centroid (~62 mm) and will clip the distal footprint, periligamentous edema, and larger/off-center knees. The ligament is also subcutaneous at the medial skin margin — an in-plane crop can trim it. Recommendation: verify crops against DICOM pixel spacing; preserve the medial femoral epicondyle, medial joint line, and ≥75 mm below the medial tibial plateau.
6. **Adjacent slices normally needed to confirm:** **No validated rule.** The 11–18 mm AP band spans ~3–6 consecutive 3–4 mm coronal slices; a focal femoral avulsion may be abnormal on only 1–2. Inspect the entire epicondyle-to-distal-tibia course; use axial to confirm level.
7. **What constitutes a positive case:** **High-grade acute tear = MRI grade II–III** (host criteria, discussion/733343): grade II — intraligamentous high signal, thickening, partial fiber disruption with some fibers continuous; grade III — full-thickness discontinuity, avulsion, wavy/retracted fibers, fluid extravasation (Vosoughi et al.).
8. **Borderline findings that stay negative or uncertain:** Grade I sprain (periligamentous edema with intact ligament) is a sprain, not a "tear" — negative under host criteria. Isolated superficial edema without fiber signal change; questionable deep-layer signal without superficial involvement.
9. **Common mimics / false positives:** MCL bursitis (fluid between superficial and deep layers, intact fibers); pes anserine bursitis/tendinopathy (more anteromedial/distal); reactive edema from medial meniscal pathology, OA, effusion, or MPFL injury; meniscocapsular separation with intact superficial MCL; Stener-like lesion (distal complete tear displaced superficial to the pes anserine tendons — itself a true positive, but easy to mislocalize).
10. **Does chronicity, degeneration, previous surgery, or image quality change interpretation?** **Yes.** Healed/chronic MCL = thickened, low-signal, continuous, edema-free — do not call acute tear. Pellegrini-Stieda calcification at the femoral attachment marks prior injury. Chronic laxity can look near-normal on static MRI. Missing FS coronal series should lower confidence substantially for grade I–II.

### Medial meniscus tear
1. **Most diagnostic plane:** **Sagittal** PD for the classic evaluation; map by region — peripheral sagittal slices show the body as a continuous bow-tie (~2 contiguous bow-ties at 4–5 mm thickness), central slices show the anterior and posterior horns as separate triangles.
2. **Preferred sequence:** **Short-TE PD** — the highest-resolution morphology sequence; in this dataset that is the non-fluid-sensitive sagittal/coronal series when present. FS PD/T2 (`Fluid_Sensitive=1`) adds conspicuity for fluid-filled clefts, marrow edema, and meniscocapsular injury (Bolog & Andreisek 2016). Do not discard sagittal T1-like series: morphology and surfacing signal remain useful.
3. **Confirmatory plane or sequence:** **Coronal** (mid = body and extrusion; anterior = anterior horn/root; posterior = posterior horn and posterior medial root) and **axial FS** for radial, parrot-beak, and root mapping. One sagittal + one coronal image satisfies the two-image rule in practice.
4. **Where anatomically to look:** Body on peripheral sagittal/mid-coronal; horns on central sagittal and anterior/posterior coronal; **posterior medial root** on posterior coronal and central sagittal, ~8 mm anterior to the superior PCL insertion. Check the tibial margin for body extrusion.
5. **Crop: is a central 130 mm crop sufficient?** **Yes.** Menisci, tibial spines, roots, and notch (where displaced fragments hide) all sit well within the central ~10 cm of a 140–160 mm FOV; a 130 mm crop loses nothing diagnostic. Verify the crop is not silently a no-op on small-FOV series (discussion/734105).
6. **Adjacent slices normally needed to confirm:** **Two-slice-touch rule**: surface-contacting signal in the same region on two images (classically two consecutive sagittal; one sagittal + one coronal acceptable). PPV ~53% with one image vs ~90% with two (De Smet & Tuite 2006). Exceptions: abnormal morphology/displaced fragment overrides; root tears may be conspicuous on 1–2 key slices per plane.
7. **What constitutes a positive case:** **Abnormal signal touching an articular surface on ≥2 images OR morphologic deformity** (host criteria, discussion/733343) — truncation, surface defect, abnormally small segment, displaced/bucket-handle fragment. Root-tear signs: ghost meniscus (absent posterior root fibrocartilage on sagittal), truncation sign (abrupt vertical fluid gap at the root on coronal), radial cleft on axial. Medial body extrusion >3 mm beyond the tibial margin is abnormal and strongly associated with posterior root tears.
8. **Borderline findings that stay negative or uncertain:** **Grade 1 (globular) and grade 2 (linear) intrameniscal signal without surface contact = negative** — intrasubstance/myxoid degeneration. Uncertain surface contact = negative under the host's borderline rule (discussion/733343).
9. **Common mimics / false positives:** Meniscocapsular separation (fluid between meniscus and capsule), meniscal flounce, chondrocalcinosis and meniscal ossicles (signal/contour alteration), magic-angle signal in curved posterior horn fibers on short-TE series, prominent pediatric vascularity. The transverse ligament pitfall is mainly an anterior-horn lateral issue.
10. **Does chronicity, degeneration, previous surgery, or image quality change interpretation?** **Yes, critically for surgery.** After <25% resection, native criteria remain reasonably accurate; after ≥25% resection or repair, surface-contacting intermediate PD/T1 signal is no longer specific (granulation, exposed grade-2 signal, healing) and repaired menisci retain high signal for years — require fluid-equivalent T2 signal entering the meniscus, a displaced fragment, or a new-location tear (Baker et al. 2018). Degenerative horizontal cleavage in older knees still counts if it reaches a surface twice, but expect lower label agreement.

### Lateral meniscus tear
1. **Most diagnostic plane:** **Sagittal** PD, same framework as medial: peripheral slices = bow-tie body, central slices = anterior/posterior horns. Note the posterior horn of the lateral meniscus slopes upward toward its root — trace it carefully to the posterolateral root near the ACL/PCL.
2. **Preferred sequence:** **Short-TE PD** (non-fluid-sensitive sagittal/coronal when present) for morphology and surface signal; FS PD/T2 adds fluid clefts and marrow/meniscocapsular conspicuity (Bolog & Andreisek 2016).
3. **Confirmatory plane or sequence:** **Coronal** (body, extrusion, roots) and **axial FS** — axial is especially valuable laterally for radial tears, the posterolateral root, and sorting out popliteus/meniscofemoral ligament mimics. Posterior lateral root: ~10.8 mm posterior to the ACL / ~12.7 mm anterior to the PCL; anterior lateral root ~4.1 mm lateral to the posterolateral ACL bundle.
4. **Where anatomically to look:** Body on peripheral sagittal/mid-coronal; horns on central sagittal and anterior/posterior coronal; **posterolateral root** on posterior coronal/central sagittal adjacent to the tibial spine. Check the lateral tibial margin for extrusion (threshold lower than medial — see #7).
5. **Crop: is a central 130 mm crop sufficient?** **Yes.** Same reasoning as medial — the lateral meniscus, roots, and notch are central structures; nothing diagnostic sits in the 5–15 mm peripheral trim of a typical 140–160 mm FOV.
6. **Adjacent slices normally needed to confirm:** **Two-slice-touch rule** as medial: surface signal on two images (~90% PPV) vs one (~53%) (De Smet & Tuite 2006); morphology/displaced fragment overrides; root tears may dominate 1–2 key slices.
7. **What constitutes a positive case:** **Surface-reaching signal on ≥2 images OR morphologic deformity** (host criteria, discussion/733343). Lateral specifics: body extrusion >~1 mm beyond the tibial margin is abnormal (vs >3 mm medial); root tear signs identical (ghost meniscus, truncation, radial cleft). In a **discoid** meniscus, require linear surface-reaching signal, deformation, or displacement — diffuse internal signal is poorly specific.
8. **Borderline findings that stay negative or uncertain:** Grade 1/2 intrasubstance signal without surface contact = negative; uncertain surface contact = negative (host borderline rule). Discoid morphology alone, without surfacing signal or deformation, is a variant, not a tear.
9. **Common mimics / false positives (lateral-rich list):** **Popliteus tendon** in its hiatus mimics a posterior horn tear or loose body; **meniscofemoral ligaments (Humphrey/Wrisberg)** running from the posterior horn to the medial femoral condyle mimic a tear or displaced fragment; **transverse intermeniscal ligament** at the anterior horn (trace it across Hoffa's fat pad); magic angle in the posterior horn; chondrocalcinosis; flounce; pediatric vascularity. **Discoid lateral meniscus** (bow-tie on ≥3 consecutive 5 mm sagittal slices, coronal body width >14–15 mm, meniscal/tibial width ratio >20%) is a trap for both overcalling tears and missing real ones amid diffuse signal; Wrisberg-type may lack normal posterior attachment.
10. **Does chronicity, degeneration, previous surgery, or image quality change interpretation?** **Yes** — postoperative rules as medial: <25% resection → native criteria usable; ≥25% resection or repair → intermediate surface signal loses specificity; require fluid-equivalent T2 signal, displaced fragment, or new-location tear (Baker et al. 2018). Lateral meniscectomy history also predisposes to lateral compartment OA — an interaction with the Lateral OA label. Thin-slice axial series materially help with radial/root calls; thick 4–5 mm sagittals inflate one-slice false positives near the popliteal hiatus.

### Medial OA
1. **Most diagnostic plane:** **Coronal** — displays the medial femoral condyle and medial tibial plateau weight-bearing cartilage en face plus marginal osteophytes, tibial spines, and meniscal extrusion.
2. **Preferred sequence:** **Coronal PD FS** (`Fluid_Sensitive=1 & Fat_Suppression=1`) is the workhorse for tibiofemoral cartilage, BMLs, and cysts; PD is the most accurate sequence for chondral lesions. Caveat: osteophytes are less conspicuous on FS (marrow fat suppressed) — they appear as marginal cortical spurs; non-FS short-TE (T1 > PD) or GRE-type series show them best (Hunter et al. 2011).
3. **Confirmatory plane or sequence:** **Sagittal** for posterior femoral condyle and posterior tibial plateau cartilage and osteophytes; non-FS coronal/sagittal (T1-like) to firm up osteophytes and sclerosis.
4. **Where anatomically to look:** Medial compartment on mid-coronal slices — apposed cartilage of the medial femoral condyle weight-bearing surface and medial tibial plateau; marginal osteophytes at the condylar and plateau rims and tibial spines. Posterior condyle/plateau on sagittal. Measure cartilage loss along the surface: host criterion is extent (≥1 cm), so scroll the full compartment.
5. **Crop: is a central 130 mm crop sufficient?** **Yes.** The medial tibiofemoral compartment, spines, and marginal osteophytes lie well inside the central FOV. The only losses are peripheral marrow and soft tissue — irrelevant to OA grading.
6. **Adjacent slices normally needed to confirm:** No formal rule; practically, **cartilage loss should persist over the ≥1 cm extent** the host criterion requires — i.e., ~3 consecutive 3–4 mm coronal slices for full-thickness loss, or confirm a focal defect on a second plane. Osteophytes: a definite spur visible on ≥2 adjacent slices avoids volume-averaging false positives.
7. **What constitutes a positive case:** **≥1 cm of high-grade (>50% thickness) cartilage loss in the medial compartment** (host criteria, discussion/733343) — i.e., modified Outerbridge grade 3–4 / WORMS partial-to-full-thickness defects (2.5/5/6), not mere signal change. MOST-style MRI definitions support this: definite cartilage damage (WORMS ≥2) plus a definite osteophyte (WORMS ≥2, ~3–5 mm+; equivocal = WORMS 1 = negative; size bands <3 / 3–5 / >5 mm; Zhang et al. 2018). Supporting features: subchondral BML or cyst, bone attrition, meniscal extrusion/degeneration.
8. **Borderline findings that stay negative or uncertain:** WORMS cartilage grade 1 (signal change, normal thickness); small focal partial-thickness defects <1 cm; equivocal (WORMS 1) or <3 mm marginal osteophytes; KL-1 equivalents; isolated Hoffa fat-pad signal; small physiologic fluid. Host rule: borderline = negative (discussion/733343).
9. **Common mimics / false positives:** Focal chondral defects from acute injury (not OA without degenerative context), osteochondritis dissecans, spontaneous insufficiency fracture (SIFK) of the medial femoral condyle presenting with marrow edema and subchondral low signal — do not relabel as OA; cartilage partial-volume thinning at the condylar edges; magic-angle cartilage signal. Joint-space narrowing on MRI tracks true cartilage loss better than osteophytes do (KL 2 with JSN: 44% WORMS ≥4 cartilage loss vs 4% without).
10. **Does chronicity, degeneration, previous surgery, or image quality change interpretation?** **Yes.** OA is inherently chronic — BMLs and effusion fluctuate but structural loss defines the label. Prior medial meniscectomy or root tear accelerates medial compartment loss; post-arthroscopy cartilage defects should still meet the ≥1 cm/>50% rule. On FS-only protocols, osteophyte conspicuity drops — lean on cartilage extent and attrition rather than spur size when no non-FS series exists.

### Lateral OA
1. **Most diagnostic plane:** **Coronal** for the lateral femoral condyle and lateral tibial plateau weight-bearing cartilage, marginal osteophytes, and lateral meniscal extrusion — the same tibiofemoral workhorse view as for the medial compartment.
2. **Preferred sequence:** **Coronal PD FS** (`Fluid_Sensitive=1 & Fat_Suppression=1`) for cartilage, BMLs, cysts; non-FS short-TE (T1/PD-like) for osteophyte conspicuity, since FS suppresses marrow fat and makes spurs less conspicuous (Hunter et al. 2011).
3. **Confirmatory plane or sequence:** **Sagittal** — posterior femoral condyle and posterior plateau cartilage and osteophytes; sagittal also captures the lateral-compartment cartilage over the condylar arc that coronal can foreshorten.
4. **Where anatomically to look:** Lateral compartment on mid-coronal slices — apposed lateral femoral condyle and lateral tibial plateau cartilage in the weight-bearing zone; lateral marginal osteophytes at the condylar and plateau rims; posterior condyle/plateau cartilage and spurs on sagittal. Check for lateral meniscal extrusion (>~1 mm) and maceration as supporting features, and scan the notch margin on coronal for lateral-sided spur formation.
5. **Crop: is a central 130 mm crop sufficient?** **Yes** — the lateral tibiofemoral compartment is central in any standard knee FOV; the 5–15 mm peripheral trim is non-diagnostic for OA.
6. **Adjacent slices normally needed to confirm:** As medial: cartilage loss must cover **≥1 cm** (≈3 consecutive 3–4 mm coronal slices) or be confirmed orthogonally; definite osteophytes should persist on ≥2 slices to exclude volume averaging.
7. **What constitutes a positive case:** **≥1 cm of high-grade (>50% thickness) cartilage loss in the lateral compartment** (host criteria, discussion/733343); MOST framework equivalent — definite cartilage damage (WORMS ≥2, ideally full-thickness 2.5/5/6) plus a definite osteophyte (WORMS ≥2, ~3–5 mm+). Supporting: BML, subchondral cyst, attrition, lateral meniscal extrusion/degenerative tear.
8. **Borderline findings that stay negative or uncertain:** Grade-1 cartilage signal change; focal partial-thickness defects <1 cm; equivocal/<3 mm osteophytes; KL-1 equivalents. Because isolated lateral osteophytes and tiny lateral defects are common in athletic knees, hold the ≥1 cm threshold strictly (host borderline rule, discussion/733343).
9. **Common mimics / false positives:** Acute osteochondral injury of the lateral femoral condyle (sulcus impaction from pivot-shift or patellar dislocation) mimicking cartilage loss; osteochondritis dissecans of the lateral condyle; discoid lateral meniscus distorting the apparent joint space; focal post-traumatic defects misread as primary OA; cartilage thinning illusion from condylar curvature/partial volume.
10. **Does chronicity, degeneration, previous surgery, or image quality change interpretation?** **Yes — more than medial.** Lateral compartment OA is less common and is frequently post-traumatic or post-meniscectomy: prior lateral meniscectomy, lateral root tear, or old osteochondral injury accelerates lateral cartilage loss, so a lateral OA label often coexists with surgical/traumatic history — this is a genuine chronicity–surgery interaction worth encoding as context, not noise. Post-resection lateral cartilage defects still must satisfy the ≥1 cm/>50% host rule; with FS-only series, under-weight osteophyte size and over-weight measurable cartilage extent and attrition.


# Section 3 — Joint Space, Fluid, Synovium & Marrow Findings

Routing reference for six of the 12 competition targets: PF OA, Effusion, Synovitis, Baker's Cyst, Contusion, Fracture. Series metadata gives only Anatomical_Plane, Fluid_Sensitive, and Fat_Suppression flags — no named sequences — so "PD FS" below means the fluid-sensitive, fat-suppressed series of the stated plane, and any non-fluid-sensitive series is a T1/PD-like morphology sequence whose contrast weighting is unknown.

## Patellofemoral OA

### Patellofemoral (PF) osteoarthritis

1. **Most diagnostic plane: axial.** Axial PD FS is the only plane that profiles the patellar facets and trochlear groove en face and shows PF margin osteophytes; it also doubles as the effusion-grading series (suprapatellar pouch). Host overview confirms patellofemoral cartilage is best assessed on axial.
2. **Preferred sequence: PD FS (fluid-sensitive, fat-suppressed flags).** PD is the most accurate conventional sequence for chondral lesions, and FS makes cartilage defects and subchondral BMLs conspicuous. Caveat: osteophytes are LESS conspicuous on FS (marrow fat suppressed) — they read as cortical/marginal spurs. If a non-fluid-sensitive axial series exists, use it for spur detection (osteophytes best on non-FS short-TE T1 > PD or GRE) (Hunter et al. 2011).
3. **Confirmatory plane/sequence: sagittal** — profiles the trochlea/anterior femur and the superior and inferior patellar poles (common spur sites) and the central weight-bearing trochlear cartilage; a non-FS sagittal series helps confirm osteophytes that FS suppresses.
4. **Where to look: axial slices through the patellofemoral joint** — lateral and medial patellar facets (lateral facet disease predominates), the trochlear groove, and the medial/lateral PF joint margins for osteophytes; sagittal for superior/inferior patellar poles and trochlea. Also grade effusion on the same axial stack.
5. **Crop: yes, central 130 mm is sufficient** for the PF compartment itself. Patella and trochlea sit centrally/anteriorly within a 140–160 mm FOV; a 130 mm crop trims only ~5–15 mm of periphery per side, which is subcutaneous fat/skin at the anterior margin. Verify the crop is centered on the joint, not the tibiofemoral notch, in off-center acquisitions.
6. **Adjacent slices: ~3–5 consecutive axial slices** span the articular patella (patellar articular height ~20–30 mm at 3–4 mm slice thickness); cartilage loss must be measurable across ≥1 cm of contiguous slices to satisfy the host rule, so single-slice defects are insufficient by definition.
7. **Positive case:** Delphi PF definition — a definite osteophyte PLUS partial- or full-thickness cartilage loss in the PF compartment; host rule (discussion/733343) — OA = ≥1 cm of high-grade (>50% thickness) cartilage loss per compartment, i.e., modified Outerbridge ≥3 sustained over ≥1 cm of patellar or trochlear surface. Borderline findings are graded negative.
8. **Borderline negatives:** WORMS cartilage grade 1 (intrachondral signal change, normal thickness); equivocal osteophytes (WORMS 1 / KL-1 equivalent); isolated marginal spurs <3 mm at patellar poles (common, often asymptomatic); Hoffa fat pad signal alone; tiny physiologic fluid.
9. **Mimics/false positives:** magic-angle or short-TE intrachondral signal without thickness loss; dorsal defect of the patella and other developmental variants; focal chondromalacia/traumatic chondral shear in young patients without osteophyte (host rule requires compartment-level OA, not isolated chondral lesions); partial-volume thinning at facet edges.
10. **Chronicity/quality:** PF OA is a chronic degenerative label — older age, trochlear dysplasia, and prior patellar dislocation shift priors up; no acute/chronic split applies. **Dataset coverage risk: axial series are the fewest (~5,898 vs ~9,864 sagittal, ~8,609 coronal), so PF OA is the most under-covered target** — build fallback inference from sagittal trochlea/patellar-pole signs and never hard-require an axial series. Motion and low in-plane resolution degrade cartilage grading; when ≥1 cm extent cannot be measured, stay negative per host borderline policy.

## Effusion

### Joint effusion

1. **Most diagnostic plane: axial.** Axial PD FS through the suprapatellar pouch is the MOAKS grading plane and shows capsular distension directly.
2. **Preferred sequence: FS PD/T2 (fluid-sensitive + fat-suppressed flags).** Simple fluid is uniformly very high signal on fluid-sensitive FS series; MRI is the most accurate modality for effusion. Non-FS series are not needed for detection.
3. **Confirmatory: midline sagittal FS** for the ACLOAS quantitative measurement — maximum AP diameter of the suprapatellar recess: grade 0 <2 mm, 1 = 2–5 mm, 2 = 5–10 mm, 3 ≥10 mm (ACLOAS). Cross-plane agreement (axial distension + sagittal diameter) hardens the label.
4. **Where to look: suprapatellar pouch/recess** (most common effusion site, ~51%), superior to the patella between prefemoral and suprapatellar fat pads; also retropatellar space on axial — fluid continuously filling it defines MOAKS ≥1.
5. **Crop: conditional — warning for sagittal.** The suprapatellar pouch extends ABOVE the patella; a tight 130 mm crop centered on the joint line can clip the superior pouch on sagittal series and truncate the AP measurement, underestimating grade. Axial in-plane crop is safe (pouch is central). Verify crop placement against DICOM FOV before trusting sagittal-derived features (discussion 734105 crop-fit warning).
6. **Adjacent slices:** measurement on the midline sagittal slice with maximal AP diameter (check 2–3 central slices); MOAKS grading across the axial slices traversing the pouch (~3–6 slices at 3–4 mm). No two-slice rule — effusion is a volume, not a focal lesion.
7. **Positive case: host criterion = moderate or large** — MOAKS 2 (slight convexity of suprapatellar bursa) to 3 (capsular distension) on axial, corresponding to ACLOAS ≥2, i.e., suprapatellar AP diameter ≥5 mm on midline sagittal (host criteria, discussion/733343).
8. **Borderline negatives:** trace/physiologic fluid; MOAKS 1 / ACLOAS 1 (2–5 mm) — graded negative by the host; fluid confined to the retropatellar space without bursal convexity.
9. **Mimics/false positives:** synovitis overlap — on non-contrast MRI, thickened synovium and fluid are both bright and inseparable (composite "effusion-synovitis"); uniform, smooth-walled fluid = effusion, irregular frond-like walls → coexisting synovitis. Mediopatellar plica: thin low-signal band within fluid, not fluid itself. Suprapatellar fat-pad partial volume at pouch margins.
10. **Chronicity/quality:** effusion is nonspecific — acute trauma, OA flares, inflammatory arthritis, and post-surgical states all produce it; chronic effusions more often contain debris/septations and coexisting synovial hypertrophy. Post-arthroscopy fluid is expected and should not be over-weighted. Poor fat suppression (fluid-isointense marrow) or motion blur can mimic or mask pouch distension — confirm fluid signal against a known fluid reference (e.g., popliteal vessels) before scoring.

## Synovitis

### Synovitis (non-contrast)

1. **Most diagnostic plane: axial** PD FS (suprapatellar pouch walls, medial/lateral gutters, retropatellar synovium), used jointly with sagittal.
2. **Preferred sequence: FS PD/T2 — with a core limitation.** On non-contrast MRI, synovial thickening and simple effusion CANNOT be reliably separated: both are hyperintense on fluid-sensitive FS series, hence the OA-literature composite "effusion-synovitis." Contrast-enhanced MRI is the OMERACT reference standard (inflamed synovium enhances, static fluid does not) and is NOT in this dataset.
3. **Confirmatory: sagittal PD FS** — Hoffa fat pad assessment and suprapatellar pouch wall profile; confirm any suspected focal synovial mass (plica, PVNS nodule) on two planes.
4. **Where to look:** suprapatellar pouch walls (mass-like thickening), medial gutter (plica, on axial), Hoffa fat pad (infrapatellar, sagittal), intercondylar notch, and posterior recesses for nodular disease.
5. **Crop: yes, sufficient.** All target synovial compartments (pouch, gutters, Hoffa, notch) lie centrally within 130 mm; only the superior pouch extreme is at risk on sagittal, as for effusion.
6. **Adjacent slices:** synovial thickening should persist across ≥2–3 contiguous slices and ideally two planes to exclude partial-volume or flow artifact; Hoffa signal grading spans the whole fat pad (MOAKS 0–3 by ≤33% / 34–66% / ≥66% involvement).
7. **Positive case (practical heuristic, low-confidence):** frond-like, villous, nodular or irregular INTERMEDIATE-signal thickening (signal below fluid, ~cartilage) lining the joint; mass-like suprapatellar wall thickening; or Hoffa fat pad hyperintensity (MOAKS Hoffa-synovitis ≥1). Given Hoffa signal is sensitive (86–97%) but poorly specific (10–38%) vs CE-MRI (Roemer et al.), and only CE-MRI correlates with histologic synovitis (Loeuille et al.), treat this as the softest of the 12 labels.
8. **Borderline negatives:** uniform smooth high fluid signal with thin walls = effusion only; indeterminate bright/heterogeneous pouch contents = "effusion-synovitis," score conservatively; equivocal Hoffa signal in a large effusion.
9. **Mimics/false positives:** synovial plica (normal thin <3 mm low-signal band in medial gutter; pathologic if ≥3 mm, intermediate/high signal, contacting MFC); PVNS/TGCT (nodular, LOW signal on BOTH T1 and T2, GRE/T2* blooming — near-pathognomonic, may erode bone); lipoma arborescens (frond-like mass following fat signal, suppresses on FS); post-surgical synovial change (irregular thickening ± susceptibility artifact, indistinguishable from recurrent synovitis without history); loose bodies, rice bodies, synovial chondromatosis.
10. **Chronicity/surgery/quality:** chronic inflammatory and post-surgical knees show the most false positives (scar and synovitis are inseparable without contrast); synovitis and effusion coexist independently — large smooth effusion argues simple fluid, effusion + irregular walls argues both. Non-FS series help only via fat-signal behavior (lipoma arborescens) or T1-low PVNS nodules. Because reference labels were radiologist-adjudicated on the same non-contrast data, model targets encode these same heuristics — expect label noise and calibrate confidence downward.

## Baker's cyst

### Baker's (popliteal) cyst

1. **Most diagnostic plane: axial.** Axial PD FS shows the diagnostic neck between the semimembranosus tendon and the medial head of gastrocnemius — the "speech-bubble"/"talk-bubble" configuration required for definitive diagnosis versus other popliteal collections.
2. **Preferred sequence: FS PD/T2 (fluid-sensitive, fat-suppressed flags).** Cyst content is uniformly high signal on fluid-sensitive FS series, low on T1-like series; may contain debris, septations, or loose bodies.
3. **Confirmatory: sagittal FS** for craniocaudal extent and rupture (fluid tracking distally along calf fascial planes with surrounding edema).
4. **Where to look: posteromedial popliteal fossa at the joint line** — the gastrocnemius–semimembranosus bursa, immediately posteromedial to the medial femoral condyle; trace any collection to its neck between the two named structures.
5. **Crop: yes for the origin — the cyst neck sits well inside a 140–160 mm FOV and any central 130 mm crop.** Two exceptions: (a) the tail of a large/ruptured cyst dissecting into the calf can run off the inferior end of the axial stack; (b) on sagittal series the cyst lies at the posterior FOV edge, where a tight crop can clip it. The diagnostic neck remains in-plane regardless.
6. **Adjacent slices:** the neck is typically demonstrable on 1–3 consecutive axial slices; scroll the full axial stack inferiorly for extent/rupture and confirm on sagittal. No slice-count rule — morphology (the neck) is the criterion.
7. **Positive case:** a fluid collection with the characteristic semimembranosus–medial gastrocnemius neck; **host criterion = moderate or large** (discussion/733343). Size by maximal cyst dimension; small physiologic bursal fluid does not qualify.
8. **Borderline negatives:** small slit-like physiologic fluid in the gastrocnemius–semimembranosus bursa (physiologic, not pathologic alone); tiny <1–2 cm simple collections without mass effect.
9. **Mimics/false positives — popliteal artery aneurysm is the critical don't-miss mimic:** variable flow signal, laminated thrombus, rim calcification, continuity with the artery, flow artifact. Also: popliteal vein varices; ganglion cysts (NO semimembranosus–gastrocnemius neck; e.g., parameniscal, proximal tibiofibular); hematoma (age-dependent signal, may be T1-bright, no neck); abscess (surrounding inflammatory change); popliteal lymph nodes (fatty hilum); other bursae (pes anserine, semimembranosus–TCL) at different locations.
10. **Chronicity/quality:** Baker's cysts are usually secondary to intra-articular pathology (OA, meniscal tear, effusion) — expect coexisting labels; chronic cysts develop septations/debris and may recur post-surgery. Rupture produces distal fluid tracking plus edema — a clinical DVT mimic; check the inferior axial slices. Motion artifact through the pulsatile popliteal artery can fake or obscure collections — verify across slices before labeling.

## Bone contusion

### Bone contusion (bone bruise)

1. **Most diagnostic plane: all three planes equally** — marrow edema is a volume lesion; use whichever fluid-sensitive FS series exist (sagittal most numerous in the dataset).
2. **Preferred sequence: FS PD/T2 or STIR-like** (fluid-sensitive + fat-suppressed flags) — edema-like signal = ill-defined high signal that does NOT completely replace marrow fat. If a non-FS series exists, it adds the T1-style correlate: geographic, non-linear low/intermediate signal without full fat replacement (Mink & Deutsch).
3. **Confirmatory: a second orthogonal FS plane.** Contusions show NO line in ANY plane — cross-plane review is precisely what separates bruise from occult fracture; classic patterns (below) also confirm the mechanism.
4. **Where to look — pattern-driven:** pivot-shift/ACL pattern = mid-lateral femoral condyle at the condylopatellar (sulcus) region + posterolateral tibial plateau (± medial posterior tibia contrecoup); patellar dislocation pattern = medial patellar facet + lateral femoral condyle; also subchondral weight-bearing surfaces in degenerative/overload BMLs.
5. **Crop: conditional.** Posterolateral tibial plateau and the patella sit near crop edges — the posterolateral plateau approaches the posterior-inferior periphery in large knees or off-center acquisitions, and the patella is anterior-peripheral on axial. A 130 mm crop centered on the joint line normally covers both, but verify posterior coverage on sagittal and anterior coverage on axial; clipped edema is unreadable edema.
6. **Adjacent slices:** edema should span multiple contiguous slices (~3+ at 3–4 mm) in a geographic region; confirm in a second plane. Single-slice signal foci are artifact until proven otherwise.
7. **Positive case:** geographic, ill-defined, NON-LINEAR high signal on FS sequences with NO discrete low-signal line, NO cortical breach, and NO contour deformity/depression — marrow edema pattern without fracture. Recognized trauma patterns strengthen the call.
8. **Borderline negatives/uncertain:** tiny reticular speckled lesions (Vellet reticular type, least severe — often below label threshold); edema immediately adjacent to a suspected line → escalate toward fracture work-up, not contusion; diffuse osteopenic or red-marrow signal without focal geography.
9. **Mimics/false positives:** occult fracture (line within edema), SIFK, osteonecrosis, stress reaction, degenerative subchondral BML of OA, red-marrow reconversion (symmetric, metaphyseal), post-surgical edema, magic-angle-free signal heterogeneity at physeal scars. The contusion-vs-fracture boundary is the main error source — see Fracture below.
10. **Chronicity/quality:** contusion signal appears ~30 h post-injury; median healing is 42 weeks (Boks AJR 2007), reticular fastest, prolonged by OA and age, some persist up to 2 years — "chronic" bruise signal does not mean acute injury. 80% shrink >50% by 12–14 weeks. Poor fat suppression mimics edema (unsuppressed marrow heterogeneity) — sanity-check fluid signal against vessels/effusion; low-resolution series blur the line/no-line distinction, biasing toward the contusion default.

## Occult fracture

### Fracture (occult)

1. **Most diagnostic plane: all three, mandatorily.** A fracture call requires the line in ≥2 planes; single-plane assessment is insufficient by definition.
2. **Preferred sequence — dataset caveat is decisive:** the T1 non-FS linear low-signal line is the MOST SPECIFIC fracture sign ("T1W images should be obtained to detect fracture lines"; PD is less reliable for the line), but the metadata only flags plane/fluid-sensitivity/fat-suppression — T1 is NOT guaranteed. If a non-fluid-sensitive series exists, treat it as the fracture-line sequence. Otherwise fracture must be INFERRED from FS PD/T2: a low-signal line within surrounding edema.
3. **Confirmatory:** the same low-signal line on ≥2 contiguous slices AND in a second orthogonal plane; cortical breach/step-off on the plane perpendicular to that cortex (coronal for tibial plateau rims, sagittal for anterior/posterior plateau, axial for patella); GRE/T2* if present (susceptibility amplifies trabecular discontinuity/hemorrhage — not standard here).
4. **Where to look:** subchondral bone parallel to the plate (SIFK — weight-bearing MFC in older adults, frequently with medial posterior root tears); tibial plateaus and femoral condyles at contusion-pattern sites; patella after dislocation; lines perpendicular to trabeculae/cortex with periosteal edema for stress fractures (proximal tibia, patella).
5. **Crop: conditional, mostly yes.** Weight-bearing plateau/condyle surfaces are central; risk sites are the plateau rims (coronal crop edges), posterior plateau (sagittal posterior edge), and patella (axial anterior edge). Verify coverage as for contusion; cortical step-offs at margins are unrecoverable if clipped.
6. **Adjacent slices: ≥2 contiguous slices (3–4 mm) AND ≥2 orthogonal planes** before calling fracture; a line seen on one slice in one plane = volume averaging/artifact → label contusion/edema. Contusions show no line in any plane.
7. **Positive case:** linear/curvilinear low-signal line within marrow edema (on T1 if available, on PD FS if not), cortical breach or step-off, or subchondral impaction/depression — confirmed per rule 6. Vellet types: reticular (serpiginous, distant from plate), geographic (confluent, plate-adjacent), linear subcortical (~2 mm wide band), stress, osteochondral (line or contour deformity disrupting cartilage + subchondral plate). Host labels adjudicated by MSK radiologists (discussion/733343).
8. **Borderline negatives:** single-slice single-plane lines; geographic non-linear edema without line or deformity (= contusion); reticular subcortical signal distant from the plate; suspected lines not reproducible orthogonally.
9. **Mimics/false positives:** volume averaging at curved cortical margins; vascular channels; physeal scars; SIFK vs osteonecrosis confusion — SIFK = thin subchondral T2-hypointense line parallel to and just below the plate + extensive BME, weight-bearing MFC, older adults, with medial root tears (subchondral low signal >4 mm thick or >14 mm long = poor prognosis), whereas osteonecrosis is serpiginous with a double-line sign; degenerative subchondral cyst/sclerosis margins; magic-angle-free chemical-shift dark rims.
10. **Chronicity/surgery/quality:** subacute healing sclerosis/callus can make the line MORE visible over weeks — a line absent acutely may appear on follow-up appearance within a study; persistent subchondral low signal + collapse suggests SIF-ON progression rather than healing. Stress-reaction edema resolves within ~6 months; grading runs periosteal edema → marrow edema on FS → edema on non-FS → discrete line. Prior surgery (drill tracts, hardware susceptibility) creates linear low signal — check for blooming artifact and geometry. Because the dataset lacks guaranteed T1, fracture is the target with the largest inherent specificity loss: default to the ≥2-slice/≥2-plane rule and accept contusion as the fallback class.

## Part 2 — Label Policy Summary + Crop Risk

### Official positive thresholds (host, discussion/733343; double-read MSK radiologists, 3rd-reader adjudication)

- **ACL**: positive = high-grade partial (>50% fibers) or full tear. Negative: intact fibers, isolated mucoid degeneration, one-slice signal only, isolated secondary signs (anterior translation, notch sign, contusion pattern).
- **MCL**: positive = high-grade acute tear (≈ MRI grade II–III: partial fiber disruption or complete discontinuity). Negative: grade I sprain/periligamentous edema with intact fibers; chronic thickened ligament without edema; bursitis.
- **Medial / Lateral Meniscus**: positive = signal touching an articular surface on ≥2 images (two-slice-touch) OR morphologic deformity (truncation, ghost root, displaced fragment, extrusion with root tear). Negative: grade 1–2 intrasubstance signal without surface contact; ligament/tendon/magic-angle mimics; postoperative intermediate signal alone.
- **Medial / Lateral / PF OA**: positive = ≥1 cm of high-grade (>50% thickness) cartilage loss per compartment. Negative: WORMS-1 signal-only change, equivocal or <3 mm isolated osteophytes, isolated BML/Hoffa signal.
- **Effusion**: positive = moderate or large (MOAKS 2–3; ≈ suprapatellar AP ≥5 mm). Negative: trace/physiologic fluid.
- **Synovitis**: positive per host criteria (moderate/large grading family); on non-contrast data treat frond-like/nodular intermediate-signal thickening as positive; uniform fluid signal alone = effusion only. Hoffa signal alone is sensitive but non-specific — label cautiously.
- **Baker's Cyst**: positive = moderate or large posteromedial cyst with diagnostic neck. Negative: small physiologic bursal fluid; neck-less collections.
- **Contusion**: positive = geographic marrow edema-like signal without line or contour deformity. Negative: anything meeting Fracture criteria.
- **Fracture**: positive = discrete low-signal line (best on T1 if present), cortical breach, or subchondral impaction, confirmed on ≥2 slices AND 2 planes. Negative: line on a single slice/plane only (→ Contusion).
- **Global**: borderline findings are graded NEGATIVE; no "abnormal" umbrella label; no PCL/LCL labels. Only 58/4,407 train studies have per-condition labels — the rest must be mined from multilingual reports.

### 130 mm crop risk summary

A 130 mm central crop is smaller than the typical 140–160 mm FOV and trims ~5–15 mm per side; centered on the joint line it reaches ~65 mm each way.

- **HIGH RISK**
  - **MCL** — tibial insertion centroid ~62 mm below the joint line (range to ~71.5 mm): a joint-line-centered 130 mm crop barely includes it and clips the distal footprint/edema in large or off-center knees; in-plane medial soft tissues sit at the skin margin. Require: full medial epicondyle, medial joint line, ≥75 mm below medial plateau.
  - **Effusion** — suprapatellar pouch extends above the patella: tight sagittal crops clip the superior pouch and under-grade effusion.
  - **Baker's cyst** — neck at the joint line is safe, but tails of large/ruptured cysts run off the inferior axial stack and posterior sagittal FOV edge.
  - **Contusion** — posterolateral tibial plateau bruises (pivot-shift pattern) lie near the lateral/posterior crop edge.
- **LOW RISK**
  - **ACL** (notch is central), **menisci** (body/horns/roots well inside), **tibiofemoral OA cartilage**, **Baker's cyst neck**, **PF OA on axial** (patella/trochlea within FOV), **fracture lines** (osseous, central).
- **Practical recommendation**: verify the crop fits per-series against DICOM PixelSpacing before cropping — the forum's "silent no-op" warning (an over-large requested crop can silently do nothing; discussion/734105). Consider 160 mm or FOV-adaptive cropping; never assume a central 130 mm crop is universally safe, especially for MCL and sagittal fluid collections.
